# 1. THE ANSWER

## The application

**PRESET//IRL** is a mobile-first PWA that lets someone photograph any real-world gear arrangement or physical setup, identify the important settings and connections, save that state as a reusable preset, and later restore it through an aligned visual overlay, human-confirmed checklist, and AI-assisted difference report.

The primitive is not “store a photo.” It is:

> **Capture reality as a versioned state that can be restored.**

Examples:

- a home-studio vocal chain;
- a guitar pedalboard and amp configuration;
- a mobile DJ rig;
- podcast or livestream equipment;
- a camera-and-lighting arrangement;
- a rehearsal-room layout;
- a stage or event setup;
- a 3D printer, espresso station, aquarium, ham-radio desk, workshop, or other expensive prosumer system;
- any collection of physical controls, connections, positions, screenshots, notes, and measurements that someone needs to recreate exactly.

The first market is deliberately narrower than the eventual product:

> **Equipment-heavy creators and serious hobbyists who repeatedly dismantle, alter, transport, share, or rebuild setups.**

That includes musicians, audio engineers, podcasters, streamers, photographers, videographers, mobile DJs, live-event technicians, makers, and high-spend hobbyists. They already spend disproportionately on gear, but much of their operational memory still lives in camera rolls, screenshots, masking tape, scattered notes, and “I swear that knob was around two o’clock.”

## Why it has the “why didn’t I think of that?” shape

Software has presets. Physical systems usually do not.

PRESET//IRL gives the physical world the missing primitive software has had for decades:

- Save
- Save As
- Version History
- Compare
- Restore
- Share

That sentence explains the product without requiring a TED Talk, ceremonial incense, or an MBA translating obvious shit into a quadrant.

# 2. WHY THIS WON THE 2026 PRESSURE TEST

## Current app climate

The global app economy remains financially healthy, but undifferentiated software is increasingly vicious territory:

- Sensor Tower reports that 2025 worldwide in-app purchase revenue reached **$167 billion**, up approximately 10% year over year; non-game app spending exceeded game spending for the first time.
- RevenueCat’s 2026 subscription analysis covers more than **115,000 apps** and **$16 billion** in revenue. Its data shows increasingly polarized outcomes rather than a comfortable middle class of average apps.
- RevenueCat also reports that AI apps can earn more per payer while churning materially faster. That argues against another generic AI chat wrapper and for an enduring workflow where AI remains an invisible labor-saving component.
- First-session value is critical. RevenueCat reports that most cancellations from very short trials happen almost immediately. PRESET//IRL therefore creates the first usable preset before asking the user to explore a sprawling feature zoo.
- Current multimodal models can compare multiple images, detect objects, return normalized bounding boxes, and produce structured output. The central technical interaction is therefore feasible without training a custom computer-vision model on day one.
- PWAs remain a practical Android-first and cross-platform route: installable from the web, capable of camera access, local storage, offline behavior, and direct web billing without requiring separate native applications for initial validation.

## Hidden paying audience

The target is not “all creators,” a phrase broad enough to include a toddler holding a crayon and a Fortune 500 video department.

The initial cross-section is:

1. **People who own expensive physical equipment.**
2. **People whose results depend on tiny physical settings or placements.**
3. **People who repeatedly change, transport, share, or rebuild that equipment.**
4. **People whose lost setup costs more than a modest subscription.**
5. **People who already buy accessories, cases, cables, presets, templates, insurance, and training.**

They have demonstrated discretionary spending, recurring setup pain, and a natural reason to return whenever a rig changes or must be restored.

## Competitor reality check

The pain is validated, but existing tools are fragmented into vertical islands:

- **PedalPlex** saves guitar/bass pedal and amp settings.
- **Session Recall** provides digital recall sheets for analog studio equipment.
- **LumiPilot** focuses on recreating photography lighting.
- **Properly, RapidEye, and ItemWise** apply visual standards or baseline comparison to property turnovers and inspections.
- Beauty passports and pet/house manuals are also already represented by products such as **SAY** and **Vadem**, making those less attractive as a fresh wedge.

**Inference:** the open opportunity is not pretending nobody has ever photographed a knob. It is turning the repeated pattern into a general product primitive—**versioned, restorable physical-state presets**—then entering through one audience Derek can understand and reach authentically.

This is not a claim that no adjacent patent, product, or unpublished startup exists. A formal trademark, patent, and competitive review is required before significant investment.

## 2. PRODUCT THESIS

People who use analog, physical, or mixed digital/physical systems lose valuable configurations because those systems lack memory. Photos are unstructured. Notes separate from images. Screenshots disappear into camera rolls. Collaborators receive incomplete instructions. Tiny changes cannot be compared across time.

PRESET//IRL converts a setup into a **State Capsule**:

- one or more baseline photos;
- optional screenshots;
- detected and user-confirmed equipment;
- user-confirmed state points such as knobs, sliders, switches, cable connections, object positions, distances, screen values, and custom notes;
- purpose and context;
- version history;
- restoration steps;
- current-versus-baseline findings;
- uncertainty labels;
- an optional temporary collaborator link.

The application is not a generic AI chat app. AI works behind the interface to reduce annotation labor. Every important AI interpretation remains editable and human-confirmed.

## 5. BRAND AND VISUAL DIRECTION

The brand should feel like precision equipment, a camera monitor, an audio rack, and version-control software had an unusually attractive child.

- dark graphite interface rather than generic blue-white SaaS;
- crisp, serious, high-contrast, tactile, and slightly futuristic;

- no gratuitous galaxy wallpaper, floating 3D blobs, cartoon robot, or purple-gradient AI slop;
- motion should communicate capture, comparison, saving, synchronization, and restoration;

2. **State Point Pin** — numbered/typed pin anchored to normalized image coordinates.
3. **Version Ghost** — translucent prior-state layer used for comparison.
4. **Match Ring** — circular progress indicator showing confirmed, changed, missing, and uncertain points.
5. **Change Receipt** — concise, exportable record of what changed and why.

### 7.8 Restore mode

This is the product’s cathedral. Make it excellent.

1. Select a preset/version.
2. Display the baseline as a translucent camera ghost.
3. Guide the user to align at least three stable anchor points.
4. Capture the current image.
5. Run client-side alignment/crop assistance.
6. Compare baseline and current images server-side through the vision adapter.
7. Display findings grouped as:
   - Matched
   - Changed
   - Missing
   - Moved
   - Uncertain
8. Never label a low-confidence finding as fact.
9. Let the user mark each finding:
   - Fixed
   - Accept change
   - False detection
   - Ignore for this session
10. Update the Match Ring live.
11. Finish with:
   - `Restored to baseline`
   - `Save as new version`
   - `Finish with accepted differences`
12. Generate a Change Receipt.

## 12. AI VISION CONTRACT

Implement two separate server-side operations:

1. `analyzeBaseline`
2. `compareCurrentToBaseline`

Validate all model output with Zod. Retry malformed structured output once with a repair prompt. If still invalid, preserve the images and move the session to manual review rather than fabricating results.

### `analyzeBaseline` input

- setup category and purpose;
- baseline image(s);
- optional detail image(s);
- optional screenshots;
- optional transcribed voice note;
- existing template vocabulary.

### `analyzeBaseline` output schema

```ts
type BaselineAnalysis = {
  summary: string;
  detectedEquipment: Array<{
    temporaryId: string;
    label: string;
    likelyCategory: string;
    bbox: [number, number, number, number];
    confidence: number;
    uncertainty?: string;
  }>;
  proposedStatePoints: Array<{
    temporaryId: string;
    type:
      | "knob"
      | "slider"
      | "toggle"
      | "connector"
      | "cable"
      | "placement"
      | "distance"
      | "screen_value"
      | "text_label"
      | "measurement"
      | "custom";
    label: string;
    observedState: string;
    bbox: [number, number, number, number];
    criticalitySuggestion: "critical" | "important" | "reference";
    confidence: number;
    requiresHumanConfirmation: true;
    uncertainty?: string;
  }>;
  proposedRestoreSteps: Array<{
    order: number;
    instruction: string;
    relatedTemporaryIds: string[];
    confidence: number;
  }>;
  privacyWarnings: string[];
  safetyWarnings: string[];
  limitations: string[];
};
```

All bounding boxes use a documented normalized coordinate order and range. Pick one convention, test it, and use it consistently.

### `compareCurrentToBaseline` output schema

```ts
type ComparisonAnalysis = {
  alignmentQuality: "good" | "usable" | "poor";
  alignmentNotes: string[];
  findings: Array<{
    statePointId?: string;
    type: "matched" | "changed" | "missing" | "moved" | "added" | "uncertain";
    description: string;
    baselineBbox?: [number, number, number, number];
    currentBbox?: [number, number, number, number];
    confidence: number;
    evidence: string[];
    suggestedAction?: string;
  }>;
  overallSummary: string;
  cannotDetermine: string[];
  safetyWarnings: string[];
};
```

### AI behavior rules

- Never infer electrical safety, voltage compatibility, signal-flow correctness, medical safety, structural safety, or code compliance.
- Never tell a user to connect or energize equipment based only on image interpretation.
- Never identify a person or perform face recognition.
- Never expose provider chain-of-thought.
- Distinguish observed, likely, uncertain, and unknown.
- Any confidence below the configured threshold must appear as `uncertain`.
- Require user confirmation before saving critical state points.
- Make it easy to report false detections.
- Store model name, request version, output schema version, latency, token/image usage, and estimated cost for debugging and cost control.

## 25. LANDING-PAGE COPY

### Hero

**Save presets for things that do not have a Save button.**

Photograph your rig, setup, controls, cables, and placements. PRESET//IRL turns them into a restorable state you can version, compare, and rebuild.

CTA: **Try the 60-second demo**

### Problem

Your camera roll remembers what it looked like. It does not remember what mattered.

PRESET//IRL attaches meaning to the picture: which knob, which port, which position, which version, and why.

### Three steps

1. **Capture it.** Photograph the full setup and the details that matter.
2. **Confirm it.** Review the state points AI proposes—or add them manually.
3. **Restore it.** Align the old state with the current one and fix only what changed.

### Trust

Private by default. Human-confirmed. Exportable. Deleteable. AI is allowed to be uncertain instead of confidently inventing a cable that leads directly into Satan’s toaster.

# 6. FIRST-DOLLAR VALIDATION PLAN

- Derek’s musician/vocalist/producer network;
